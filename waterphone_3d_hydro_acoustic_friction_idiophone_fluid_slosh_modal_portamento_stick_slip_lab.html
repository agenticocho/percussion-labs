1. Clamped-Free Bronze Cantilever Rods ($1 : 6.27 : 17.55$)

Around the rim of the stainless-steel pan, bronze rods of alternating lengths are welded at their base. Acting as clamped-free Euler–Bernoulli beams ($\beta_n L = \{1.875, 4.694, 7.855\}$), their second and third transverse modes sit at $6.267 f_1$ and $17.548 f_1$—allowing a bow stroke near the tip to leap from a warm fundamental into an eerie metallic shriek.

2. Internal Water Mass-Loading & Slosh Portamento

Inspired by the Tibetan water drum, a small fill of water inside the resonator shifts across the bottom plate's vibrational antinodes as the player tilts the cylindrical neck. The moving fluid mass dynamically alters the effective modal stiffness/mass ratio, creating natural microtonal pitch bends, vibrato, and aquatic chorus shimmer.

3. Superball Friction Mallets & Cetacean Calls

Dragging a high-friction rubber Superball mallet across the bottom stainless-steel pan excites sub-harmonic stick-slip vibrations that couple through the water layer—producing haunting whale-like glissandi used by Jim Nollman to communicate with wild orcas.

4. Classic Models & Hyperstellar Innovations
