Percussion Psychoacoustics — How the Cochlea & Auditory Cortex Hear Vibration

Greenwood Tonotopic Map • Glasberg & Moore $\text{ERB}_N$ • Zwicker Upward Spread • Temporal & Virtual Pitch

1. Upward Spread of Simultaneous Masking

Sound waves enter the fluid-filled cochlea at the stiff high-frequency base and travel toward the flexible low-frequency apex ($f(x) = 165.4(10^{2.1x}-0.88)\text{ Hz}$). Because low-frequency traveling waves must pass through higher-frequency regions before peaking and cutting off steeply, a loud bass drum or taiko creates a shallow high-frequency excitation tail ($S_U = [-24 - 0.23/f + 0.2 L_M]\text{ dB/Bark}$) that easily buries higher-pitched percussion, whereas high-pitched tones cannot mask lower ones.

2. Forward & Backward Temporal Masking

Auditory masking persists in time even when two percussion hits do not overlap physically. After a loud impact, inner hair cell neurotransmitter depletion and auditory-nerve adaptation keep the threshold elevated for $100\text{–}200\text{ ms}$ (Forward Masking, $\tau_{\text{fwd}} \approx 65\text{ ms}$), burying ghost notes played too soon after a downbeat. Even more counterintuitively, a loud hit can mask a faint click played up to $20\text{ ms}$ before it (Backward Pre-Masking) due to faster neural latency of intense stimuli.

3. Missing Fundamental & Virtual Pitch

Orchestral chimes (tubular bells) and timpani radiate strong overtones in ratios near $2:3:4:5$ while their actual fundamental $f_0$ is weak, mistuned, or absent. Yet we hear a clear pitch at $f_0$ (Virtual / Residue Pitch). As Licklider (1954) proved, even when you inject low-frequency masking noise that completely buries the $f_0$ place on the basilar membrane, the phantom pitch at $f_0$ remains audible because the brain extracts periodicity from the upper resolved harmonics.

4. Critical Bandwidth ($\text{ERB}_N$) & Outer Hair Cells

Each auditory filter has an Equivalent Rectangular Bandwidth $\text{ERB}(f) = 24.7(4.37 f_{\text{kHz}} + 1)\text{ Hz}$ ($\approx 0.9\text{ mm}$ along the basilar membrane). Active electromotility of cochlear Outer Hair Cells (OHCs) sharpens filter tuning by $2\text{–}3\times$ and generates nonlinear cubic difference tones ($2f_1 - f_2$). Reducing OHC gain broadens critical bands and causes severe upward masking swamping.
