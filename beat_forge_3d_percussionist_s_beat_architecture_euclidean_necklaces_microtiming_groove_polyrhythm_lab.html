The Mathematics & Physics of Percussion Beats & Groove

1. Euclidean Rhythms $E(k, n)$ (Toussaint 2005)

Computer scientist Godfried Toussaint proved that Bjorklund's neutron-accelerator timing algorithm—which distributes $k$ pulses across $n$ time steps as equidistantly as possible via Euclid's GCD algorithm—generates almost all foundational world percussion timelines: $E(3,8)$ Cuban tresillo, $E(5,8)$ cinquillo, $E(5,16)$ Bossa Nova, and $E(7,12)$ West African Ashanti bell patterns.

2. Microtiming Pocket & Laid-Back Snare Delay

Human groove lives in systematic millisecond deviations from the rigid grid (Keil's participatory discrepancies). Empirical studies (Danielsen et al., 2015; JASA 2026) show that laid-back funk and neo-soul drummers delay snare backbeats by $+15\text{ to }+28\text{ ms}$ while pushing kick downbeats slightly early, hovering right near the human Just Noticeable Difference threshold ($\approx 19\text{–}24\text{ ms}$).

3. Non-Isochronous Swing (MPC & J Dilla Time)

Rather than dividing 16th notes equally ($50:50$) or into rigid triplets ($66.7:33.3$), Roger Linn's MPC swing and J Dilla's unquantized drum programming explore intermediate quintuplet ($58\text{–}60\%$) and septuplet ($57\%$) subdivision ratios combined with independent voice phase offsets.

4. Ghost Notes & Polyrhythmic Phase Cycles

Master funk drummers (Clyde Stubblefield, Bernard Purdie, Zigaboo Modeliste) weave $-14\text{ dB}$ ghost-note snare taps between accented rimshot backbeats. When tracks use different step lengths $n_i$ (polymeter) or cross-divisions $p:q$ (polyrhythm), they trace phase orbits that realign every $\text{LCM}(n_1,\dots,n_6)$ pulses.
