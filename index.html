Percussion Series

Forty-one percussion laboratories,
organised by what they are.

Each lab is a self-contained instrument built from a different piece of physics or mathematics — cymatics, cavitation, gongs, tuning forks, singing bowls, chimera oscillators, solitons, isospectral drums, p-adic trees, aperiodic tilings. Turn a knob and the underlying model responds; the sound is generated from the model, not sampled over it. No build step, no backend, no account. Twenty-eight instruments grouped by family, eight experiments grouped by mechanism, and two labs that are about the whole collection.

Metronomes on a shared beam that synchronise through nothing but their own recoil — Huygens' 1665 observation, integrated rather than scripted. Two identical metronomes settle into ANTI-PHASE, not unison: they alternate, and the double-time pulse on the interval histogram is the physics telling you so. Sweep the coupling and watch the order parameter cross over.

BEAT-FIELD 3D

Two pulse trains and one piece of arithmetic. Detune them and the beat rate becomes a tempo; set them to 3:2 and you get a hemiola that never drifts. Then sweep the master tempo past 20 Hz and the same ratio stops being a cross-rhythm and becomes a chord — a perfect fifth at 702 cents, from the identical integers.

SAND-PILE 3D

A Bak–Tang–Wiesenfeld pile that grows until it collapses, and the collapse onsets are the beat. No tempo is set — the rhythm is the pile's own instability, bursty and clustered with quiet refractory gaps. Flip to the Poisson null and the difference is instant: human musical timing is 1/f too, which is why a grid sounds stiff and a pile does not.

DRIP-CLOCK 3D

A dripping faucet as a rhythm you can derive on paper: Tate’s law gives the drop mass, so the tempo is set by surface tension, neck radius and flow, to within a few percent. Sweep the flow and the drip stops being metronomic: period-2 is swing, then period-4, then deterministic chaos, then jetting. The only lab here whose tempo has a closed form, and it still finds a way to break it.

Percussion Labs · forty-one labs, organised by kind · built for the browser

Everything runs client-side. Audio starts after your first click or key press, because browsers require a user gesture before a page may produce sound. The modules reference Tailwind, three.js and Google Fonts from public CDNs; nothing else is fetched.
